Coast · Mangaluru

Blast and fire at MRPL refinery near Jokatte: one dead, nine hurt

A high-pressure cold separator ruptured in the Coker Hydrotreater Unit around noon on Wednesday, MRPL said.

A blast followed by a fire hit the Coker Hydrotreater Unit of Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Limited (MRPL) near Jokatte at about 12.15 pm on Wednesday, 30 September, while testing was under way, according to news reports. MRPL said a high-pressure cold separator had ruptured, causing the fire.

Reports say one person died and nine were injured. The person who died was a 30-year-old contract worker from Mulki who was first reported missing; his body was found later. Rajesh Baptist, from Surathkal, suffered burns over about 35 per cent of his body and is in the intensive care unit at Srinivas Hospital, where doctors say he is out of danger. The other injured were given outpatient treatment and medical checks.

Early reports quoted the Police Commissioner as saying eight people were hurt and the fire was under control; later reports raised the count to nine injured and one death. Nearby areas felt the impact of the explosion. This paper will follow official statements as they come.

Places · a coast feature

St Mary's Islands: the rock that looks like stacked pencils

Off the coast at Malpe, near Udupi, four small islands rise from the Arabian Sea. The most visited is Coconut Island, where visitors arrive by ferry from the harbour.

The islands are known for their columns of basalt, packed side by side and often six-sided. Geologists link them to volcanic activity tens of millions of years ago, and the site is listed among India's national geological monuments.

There is a tradition that the explorer Vasco da Gama landed here in 1498 and set up a cross, and the name of the islands is said to come from that visit. The ferries run in the fair-weather season and are usually stopped during the monsoon, when the sea is rough.

St. Mary's Islands, Wikipedia

ಮಳೆಯ ರಾತ್ರಿ

ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿನಿಂದ ಹೊರಟ ಬಸ್ಸು ಕಡಲಕೆರೆಯ ನಿಲ್ದಾಣದಲ್ಲಿ ನಿಂತಾಗ ಸಂಜೆ ಏಳು ಗಂಟೆ. ಮಳೆ ಮೂರು ದಿನದಿಂದ ಬಿಡದೆ ಸುರಿಯುತ್ತಿತ್ತು. ಅನನ್ಯಾ ಸೂಟ್‌ಕೇಸ್ ಇಳಿಸಿಕೊಂಡು ಕೊಡೆ ಬಿಚ್ಚಿದಳು. ಆದರೆ ಗಾಳಿ ಕೊಡೆಯನ್ನು ಅವಳಿಗಿಂತ ಬಲವಾಗಿ ಹಿಡಿದೆಳೆಯಿತು.

“ಅನನ್ಯಮ್ಮಾ!” ಎಂದು ದೂರದಿಂದ ಕರೆ ಬಂತು. ಹಳದಿ ರೇನ್‌ಕೋಟ್ ಹಾಕಿಕೊಂಡ ವೃದ್ಧೆಯೊಬ್ಬರು ಕೈ ಬೀಸುತ್ತಿದ್ದರು. ಕಾವೇರಿ ಅಕ್ಕ. ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತು ವರ್ಷದಿಂದ ಅಜ್ಜನ ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡಿದವರು. “ಎಷ್ಟು ಬೆಳೆದುಬಿಟ್ಟಿದ್ದೀಯಾ ಮಗಳೇ,” ಎನ್ನುತ್ತಾ ಅವರ ಕಣ್ಣು ತುಂಬಿತು.

ಅಜ್ಜ ರಾಮಣ್ಣ ಶೆಟ್ಟಿ ತೀರಿಕೊಂಡು ಹನ್ನೆರಡು ದಿನ ಆಗಿತ್ತು. ಅನನ್ಯಾ ಕೊನೆಯ ಬಾರಿ ಕಡಲಕೆರೆಗೆ ಬಂದದ್ದು ಎಂಟು ವರ್ಷದ ಹಿಂದೆ. ಆಗ ಅಜ್ಜ ಹೇಳಿದ್ದರು, “ಮನೆಯ ದೀಪ ಎಂದೂ ಆರಬಾರದು ಮಗಳೇ.”

ಮನೆಯ ಹೆಸರು ಸಮುದ್ರ ನಿಲಯ. ಕಡಲ ದಡದ ಮೇಲೆ ಎರಡಂತಸ್ತಿನ ಹಂಚಿನ ಮನೆ. ಮಾಳಿಗೆಯ ಮೇಲೆ ದೊಡ್ಡ ಕಂಚಿನ ದೀಪ. ಅದು ರಾತ್ರಿ ಮೀನುಗಾರರ ದೋಣಿಗಳಿಗೆ ದಾರಿ ತೋರಿಸುತ್ತಿತ್ತು. ಆದರೆ ಇಂದು ಆ ದೀಪ ಕತ್ತಲಲ್ಲಿತ್ತು.

ಹೊಸ್ತಿಲಲ್ಲಿ ಒಬ್ಬ ಮಹಿಳೆ ನಿಂತಿದ್ದರು. ಚಿಕ್ಕಮ್ಮ ಸುಮಿತ್ರಮ್ಮ. ಮುಖದಲ್ಲಿ ನಗುವಿಲ್ಲ. “ಬಂದೆಯಾ,” ಎಂದರು ಅಷ್ಟೇ. “ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿ ಮಲಗು. ನಾಳೆ ವಕೀಲರು ಬರ್ತಾರೆ.”

ಆ ರಾತ್ರಿ ಹಳೆಯ ಮಂಚದ ಮೇಲೆ ಮಲಗಿದಾಗ, ಕಡಲ ಸದ್ದು ಗೋಡೆ ದಾಟಿ ಒಳಗೆ ಬರುತ್ತಿತ್ತು. ಈ ಮನೆ ತನ್ನಿಂದ ಏನೋ ಮುಚ್ಚಿಡುತ್ತಿದೆ ಎಂದು ಅನನ್ಯಾಗೆ ಅನಿಸಿತು.

ಮುಂದಿನ ಸಂಚಿಕೆ ನಾಳೆ ಇದೇ ಪುಟದಲ್ಲಿ.

ಕರಾವಳಿಯ ಕಡಲಕೆರೆ ಎಂಬ ಕಾಲ್ಪನಿಕ ಊರಿನ ಕಥೆ. ಇದರಲ್ಲಿರುವ ಎಲ್ಲ ಪಾತ್ರಗಳು ಮತ್ತು ಘಟನೆಗಳು ಕಾಲ್ಪನಿಕ.

Episode 1 · The Tide Ledger

The Man with the Wet Umbrella

The monsoon had been sulking over Mangaluru for a week, and by six o'clock the newsroom of The Coastal Courier smelled of wet paper and stale tea. Ira Rao was typing up a story about a pothole that had swallowed an auto-rickshaw. It was, she thought, the most exciting thing that would happen to her that day.

Then the door opened, and an old man walked in with an umbrella that had lost the argument with the wind. Water ran off his shirt onto the floor. He looked around the room the way a person looks in a bus station, checking every face.

'Miss Rao?' he said. 'Ira Rao? You wrote about the diesel queue at Malpe.'

'Three months ago,' said Ira. 'Nobody read it.'

'I did.' He set a plastic bag on her desk. Inside was a ledger with a cloth cover, the corners soft as bread. 'My name is Anthony Pinto. I was a clerk at the Sasi Fishermen's Cooperative for thirty-one years. Do not read this here.'

'Where should I read it?'

'Somewhere the sea cannot hear.' He tried to smile and did not manage it. Then he picked up his broken umbrella and left, leaving a small lake behind him.

Ira looked at the ledger. Then she looked at the clock. Then, because she was twenty-six and curious and had never once done what she was told, she slid it into her bag and went home.

Three plants a day for a home on the coast: laterite soil, salty air and months of rain.

Udupi Mallige

ಮಲ್ಲಿಗೆJasminum sambac
Local prideGI taggedFragrant

Also called Shankarpura mallige, after the villages near Udupi where it is grown in home gardens. The laterite soil and humid air of this coast give it a strong scent, white colour and large flowers, which is why it carries a Geographical Indication tag. Buds open within five to six hours, so they are picked and strung before they bloom.

Light
Full sun for steady flowering.
Water
Regular, but never standing in water. Let excess rain drain away.
Soil
Rich, well-drained laterite soil suits it best.
Feed
Organic manure through the growing months.
Harvest
Wrap the flowers in banana leaf and sprinkle with water. They keep for up to 10 days, though the scent fades.
Watch out
Waterlogged roots in heavy rain. Raise pots on bricks.

Aboli

ಕನಕಾಂಬರCrossandra infundibuliformis
Udupi favouriteFlowers all year

Firecracker flower in English, Kanakambara in Kannada and Aboli in Konkani and Marathi. Its orange and coral blooms are strung into garlands across the coast.

Light
Bright morning sun, with cover from harsh afternoon sun.
Water
Thoroughly, whenever the top few inches of soil are dry. Under-watering harms it.
Soil
Fertile, well-drained red loam, pH 6 to 7.5.
Climate
Warm and humid, 20 to 30 degrees C. It suits Mangaluru.
Feed
Liquid fertiliser every two weeks in the growing season.

Hibiscus

ದಾಸವಾಳHibiscus rosa-sinensis
Blooms most of the yearEasy

The coast's everyday flower, seen on temple offerings and garden fences. It blooms through most of the year in warm, humid weather and grows well in pots or beds.

Light
Full sun, five to six hours. It drops buds in deep shade.
Water
When the top inch is dry. In heavy rain, let it dry between waterings.
Soil
Well-drained. Mix coarse sand into lateritic garden soil.
Feed
A light feed every two to three weeks while it flowers.
Prune
Trim leggy stems after a flush of flowers.

This month in the garden

  • Check every pot has drainage holes, and clear the saucers after rain.
  • Mulch bare soil to stop splash and keep roots steady.
  • Give ferns and balcony plants airflow to avoid fungus.
  • Sow spinach, amaranth and fenugreek, which enjoy the humidity.
  • Shield young plants from strong wind and heavy downpours.
  • Mix coarse sand into laterite soil so roots do not sit wet.
